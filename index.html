WAIVER WIRE: 7 TARGETS 🎯

Kimani Vidal emerged as the running back to target off the wire for the Chargers after Omarion Hampton's injury. (Photo by Chris Arjoon/Icon Sportswire via Getty Images)

The best fantasy managers understand how important it is to grind the waiver wire every week. You need to eat your veggies. Let's give you a look at the top of the free-agent market as we get ready for 7.

Note the names at each position are ranked in order of interest.

RUNNING BACKS

  • Kimani Vidal, Chargers (31%): He ran wild at Miami while Hassan Haskins couldn't get going. Not every week will be Dolphins-easy, but we know Omarion Hampton is out at least three more weeks.
  • Tyler Allgeier, Falcons (35%): Bye-week season is all about managing expectations, and Allgeier is doing enough to push into the flex conversation this month. He has double-digit carries in five games, and he's scored a touchdown in three of four games. Bijan Robinson is a god, but Allgeier is pretty solid, too.
  • Bam Knight, Cardinals (12%): A short touchdown bailed out a low-efficiency day at Indianapolis, but any back with a decent shot at double-digit touches needs to be considered. The Packers are a difficult Week 7 draw, however.

WIDE RECEIVERS

  • Josh Downs, Colts (49%): Sitting just inside the 50% cutline, Downs deserves mention as a key member of a steady Indianapolis passing game. He's had seven targets or more in two straight games, and that's a fantasy currency.
  • Kayshon Boutte, Patriots (9%): Boutte has been inconsistent but is also coming off a WR4 performance and Tennessee is a juicy Week 7 matchup. With Drake Maye playing so well, we need to be open minded in New England.
  • Tre Tucker, Raiders (48%): I realize the reluctance to trust anything with the Raiders right now, but Tucker has been productive in four of his six starts. Deep targets will have a lot of variance, but Tucker could be WR3 viable even against Kansas City this week.

TIGHT ENDS

  • Harold Fannin Jr., Browns (27%): He showed chemistry with fellow rookie Dillon Gabriel last week (7-81-0), and more opportunity could be coming as David Njoku battles a knee injury.
  • Cade Otton, Buccaneers (12%): He's generally a low-ceiling player, but Otton's role has bumped recently (9-132-0 over two weeks) as the Buccaneers deal with cluster injuries at wide receiver.
  • AJ Barner, Seahawks (15%): Jaxon Smith-Njigba dominates the target share in Seattle, but Barner could be an interesting side option. Barner has quietly collected 13 catches and three touchdowns over the past three games.

QUARTERBACKS

  • Jaxson Dart, Giants (32%): His passing has been hit or miss, but he's run for better than 50 yards in every start. Konami Code for the win. It's added up to QB11, QB16 and QB3 finishes. Denver is a challenge this week, but Dart belongs on fantasy rosters.

3 INTRIGUING STATS FROM WEEK 6

Joe Flacco elevated the Bengals offense in his first start for his new team in Week 6. (Photo by Michael Reaves/Getty Images)

Joel Smyth provides three different numbers from Week 6 that are both eye-opening and may go overlooked.

30.9 The amount of half-PPR fantasy points for Brian Thomas Jr. if Travis Hunter didn't line up offside. It might not be perfect, but it's coming back together for the sophomore receiver in a new offense. I fully expect the touchdowns to continue and the fantasy points to keep coming after his poor start to the season.

35-12 The Chargers' RB snaps in favor of Kimani Vidal once he broke off a 38-yard run. Before that moment at the end of the first quarter, Hassan Haskins and Vidal had eight snaps each. Vidal's hot hand got hotter as he continued to provide chunk plays for a struggling Chargers offense.

48.4 Half-PPR receiving fantasy points provided by Joe Flacco. Jake Browning's average as a starter was 33.7. It wasn't Joe Burrow, but it was better. The most important factor was previously mentioned, being that 20 of 40 targeted pass attempts went to either Ja'Marr Chase or Tee Higgins.
